On the Weight of Linen

October 3, 2026 6 min read Written by Amélie Rousseau

Every autumn we make a decision that most brands never think twice about: how heavy should the linen be. It sounds like a small question. It is not.

A lighter linen drapes beautifully on the hanger and photographs well in bright daylight, but it wears thin at the elbow within a year and rarely improves with age. A heavier gauge — the 220 gsm we've settled on for our overshirts — resists creasing at the seams, holds its structure through a wash cycle, and genuinely softens rather than degrades over repeated wear.

We source ours from a small mill outside Normandy that has spun the same fibre for four generations. It costs nearly twice what a mass-market linen supplier would charge. We have never regretted the difference.

"The right weight of cloth is the one you forget you're wearing by the second hour, and remember fondly by the tenth year."

There is a temptation, especially for a young studio, to chase what looks good in a single photograph. We try instead to ask what a garment will feel like on a body that has worn it two hundred times. That question has shaped nearly every material decision the studio has made since it opened.

Amélie Rousseau Founder & Creative Director, Atelier Lumen
